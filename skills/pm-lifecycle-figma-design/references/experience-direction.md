# Experience direction

Turn ProductSpec into an experience before choosing components.

## Design contract

Before the first write, establish:

- **Promise:** the one feeling or outcome the journey should deliver.
- **Narrative curve:** what the user knows, decides and feels at each screen.
- **Signature moment:** one product-specific composition that could not belong to a generic form.
- **System moments:** routine controls where ZDS should stay visually familiar.
- **State plan:** default, progress, success and at least one meaningful recovery state.

Do not put this contract into a detached annotation card unless the task asks for documentation. Use it to drive the screens.

## Screen choreography

Each screen needs a distinct job:

- Entry screens establish value and confidence before asking for data.
- Consent screens explain why, scope and control; they are not legal-text dumps.
- Input screens make the current task dominant and recovery nearby.
- Progress screens show what is happening and why waiting is safe.
- Success screens hand the user into a real next product state, not a generic checkmark dead end.

Vary composition by job. A repeated header + three cards + bottom button skeleton across every screen is a failed journey even when spacing is clean.

## Visual direction

- Choose one primary action color, one calm surface family and semantic success/warning/error colors.
- Use scale and whitespace to establish hierarchy before adding containers.
- Reserve cards for content that is genuinely grouped or actionable.
- Keep one strong focal area per screen.
- Use realistic Vietnamese names, dates, values and state copy.
- Avoid labels that describe the wireframe, lifecycle phase or component type.

## Creative freedom

ZDS constrains interaction semantics, not the entire composition. Custom typography, frames, shapes, masks, illustrations, charts and product-specific visual moments are allowed inside the approved Page. Do not detach or redraw a ZDS control merely to change its appearance.
